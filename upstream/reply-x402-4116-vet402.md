Your point is the sharpest thing in this thread: a receipt carried in the HTTP response cannot help when the response never comes or is an error. And your fix — accepting only a transfer carrying the attempt's one-time value, the memo, because amount and payTo are not enough when you pay the same seller the same price many times — is the same shape we arrived at from the other side.

We built the buyer-side half of this. It is installable now, no clone needed:

    npx sycee-verify --example

The idea: the buyer records a witness row of what it actually observed — what it sent, what came back (including "nothing within 20s" and "502"), how long it waited — without claiming anything about settlement or the seller's refusal reason. Settlement stays a chain read. The buyer's row and the seller's row are then reconciled into four outcomes: `match`, `status_disagrees`, `only_buyer`, `only_seller`. Crucially `only_seller` is not treated as evidence of misconduct — it equally means the buyer never had logging on. When accounts disagree, the verifier names which of nine identity members diverged, rather than just reporting two different hashes.

The identity is a fixed-order hash of method, origin, path, query, payTo, asset, network, amount, scheme — the same fields your memo argument draws on, plus the request shape. `presentation` is deliberately excluded, so a first attempt and its replay are one attempt, which is exactly the pair your 37-vs-39 gap is made of.

I am not proposing this as the delivery-receipt scheme in the RFC — a signed commitment to the response payload is a different and useful thing, and for attempts that return a body it is the stronger artifact. I am saying there is a class of attempts where no body and no receipt ever arrive, and for those the buyer has to tie a chain transfer to its own attempt unaided. That class needs a buyer-side record, and this makes one checkable.

Concretely, if it is useful: point the verifier at one of your settled-without-response attempts and its seller row, if you hold one, and see whether the two reconcile. If they do not, that is a finding we would want to hear about too. Happy to run our side against the export.csv you already publish.

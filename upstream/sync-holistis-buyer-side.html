I closed the loop I left open, and it changed one of the things I told you.

You said: you take the reader who arrives after something went wrong, I take the seller deciding whether to retry. The seller half cannot produce a comparison by itself — one ledger has nothing to disagree with. So the buyer half exists now: an agent runtime that composes the attempt identity locally from the request it sent and the `accepts[]` element it received, keeps the 402 refusal receipt instead of discarding it, and reconciles the two ledgers into `match`, `status_disagrees`, `only_buyer`, `only_seller`. It reports the asymmetry. It does not say which side is right.

Two corrections of my own, both of which you would have found:

1. The 402 challenge now carries `scheme`, `network`, `payTo`, `asset`, `amount`. Before, the buyer had no way to compute the identity the seller recorded — it could only be told one, and a told identity makes every comparison pass by construction, which is the failure mode you are building the verifier against, one level up.
2. `presentation` was counted per proof, not per attempt. Two purchases of one URL both got presentation 1, and a refusal under the second was dropped as a contradiction. That is the class of bug your vectors exist to catch, and it was in my ledger.

The honest part: the 4/4 our demo prints is our own buyer against our own seller, in one process. It shows the code path exists. It does not show two parties agreeing. Still one author. What changed is that a second party can now produce the other half without being trusted for it.

One thing about your eight vectors, which I would rather say than have you find. They are at the payload-digest layer, and the identity no longer includes a payload digest. It is over the request — method, origin, path, query, and the payment terms — because a caller holding a settlement and the request, but not our serialisation, has to be able to recompute it. That is a deliberate move away from `sha256(payload)`, not a judgement on the work: if a row ever carries a body digest, your four rules are exactly what that digest would have to satisfy. One layer down, not wasted.

-- Rachel

我把上次留下的那个口子补上了，过程中改掉了一处我跟你说过的话。

你说：你拿「出事后才来的人」，我拿「正在决定要不要重试的卖家」。卖家这一半自己凑不出比较——只有一本账，没有可对的东西。所以买家那一半现在有了：agent 运行时用「自己发出去的请求」加「收到的 accepts[]」在本地算 attempt identity，收到 402 时把那封拒绝凭证留下来（原来是直接扔掉的），再把两本账按 identity 和 presentation 对成 match / status_disagrees / only_buyer / only_seller。它只报不对称，不判谁对。

我自己两处错，你迟早会挑出来：

1. 402 账单现在带 scheme、network、payTo、asset、amount。之前买家没办法算出卖家记下的那个 identity，只能被告知一个——而被给的 identity 会让每一次比较都自动通过，这正是你验的东西的上一层版本。
2. presentation 原来是按 proof 计数，不是按 attempt。同一个 URL 买两次，两行都是 presentation 1，第二笔如果被拒，那一行会被当成矛盾丢掉。你那套向量要抓的就是这类错，而它在我账本里。

还有一句实话：demo 打出来的 4/4，是我们自己的买家对我们自己的卖家，同一个进程里。它证明代码路径存在，不证明两方一致。还是一只手。变的是：第二方现在能自己产出另一半，而且不需要被信任。

关于你那八份向量，我宁愿自己说，也不想你后来发现。它们在 body 摘要那一层，而 identity 不再含 body 摘要了。它算在请求上——method、origin、path、query，加上付款条款——因为一个拿着结算记录和请求、但没有我们序列化方式的人，必须能自己重算出来。这是刻意从 sha256(payload) 走开，不是对那份工作的评价：如果哪天某一行要带 body 摘要，你那四条规则就是它必须满足的东西。往下移了一层，不是白做。

—— Rachel
